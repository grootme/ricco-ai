No official pipeline tools, but workflows can be exported/imported.</li><li><strong>Dify</strong>: <strong>Open source community edition</strong> and commercial cloud. Self-hosting requires backend Python service, frontend Next.js, and optional vector DB. Docker Compose deployment; min. 2-core 4GB. AWS templates for enterprise deployment. No explicit CI/CD, but <span class=\"glossary-term\">container<div class=\"glossary-tooltip-box\"><h4>Container</h4><p>A standard unit of software that packages up code and all its dependencies so the application runs</p></div></span> images and env config allow upgrades.</li><li><strong>FastGPT</strong>: <strong>Community and commercial editions</strong>. Monorepo with frontend/backend code. <span class=\"glossary-term\">Helm Chart<div class=\"glossary-tooltip-box\"><h4>Helm Chart</h4><p>A package of templates and configuration used to define, install, and upgrade Kubernetes</p></div></span> for K8s and Docker deployment. 4-core 8GB server recommended. Open source for internal use, but <strong>no unauthorized SaaS</strong>. Commercial edition for SaaS deployment.</li><li><strong>RAGFlow</strong>: Default self-hosting via pip install or Docker image. Min. 4-core 16GB recommended. Can be packaged as backend service. Apache2.0 license allows free commercial use.</li><li><strong>LangGraph</strong>: Framework library, not a deployable service. Self-hosting means deploying your LangGraph-based app. MIT license, free for commercial integration.</li><li><strong>Fabric</strong>: CLI program installable anywhere. MIT license, free for enterprise use. Lightweight, resource usage depends on AI API and task complexity.</li></ul><p>Most projects offer Docker/<span class=\"glossary-term\">K8s<div class=\"glossary-tooltip-box\"><h4>K8s</h4><p>Common abbreviation for Kubernetes, derived from the 8 letters between 'K' and 's'.</p></div></span> support and moderate deployment difficulty. <strong>n8n</strong> and <strong>Dify</strong> are very self-hosting friendly; <strong>Coze Studio/Loop</strong> are more complex; <strong>FastGPT</strong> restricts SaaS use; <strong>RAGFlow</strong>/LangGraph are developer libraries; <strong>Fabric</strong> is the lightest. All can run in private environments for data ownership and customization.</p><h2 id=\"license-restrictions\">License Restrictions</h2><p>Comparing license restrictions involves understanding each open source license’s impact on commercial use, self-hosting, SaaS, and secondary development.</p><ul><li><strong>Coze Studio &amp; Loop</strong>: <strong>Apache License 2.0</strong>, no extra restrictions. Free for commercial use, modification, and distribution.</li><li><strong>n8n</strong>: <strong>Sustainable Use License</strong> (Fair-code). Free for internal use, <strong>no unauthorized commercial SaaS</strong>. Not OSI-approved open source; commercial licensing required for SaaS.</li><li><strong>Dify</strong>: <strong>Apache-2.0-based commercial-friendly license</strong>. Community edition is open source, but may restrict unauthorized SaaS. Commercial support available.</li><li><strong>FastGPT</strong>: <strong>FastGPT Open Source License</strong> (Apache2.0-based with extra terms): free for backend commercial use, <strong>no unauthorized SaaS</strong>. Commercial license available.</li><li><strong>RAGFlow</strong>: <strong>Apache License 2.0</strong>. Free for commercial use and modification.</li><li><strong>LangGraph</strong>: <strong>MIT license</strong>. Free for commercial use and modification.</li><li><strong>Fabric</strong>: <strong>MIT license</strong>. Free for commercial use and modification.</li></ul><p><strong>Coze Studio/Loop, RAGFlow, LangGraph, Fabric</strong> use permissive licenses, fully suitable for commercial use. <strong>Dify, FastGPT, n8n</strong> add “no unauthorized SaaS” restrictions. Choose based on your business model and compliance needs.</p><h2 id=\"community-activity--maintenance-status\">Community Activity &amp; Maintenance Status</h2><p>Community activity and maintenance status are reflected in GitHub stars, team investment, contributions, update frequency, and long-term support.</p><ul><li><strong>Coze Studio &amp; Loop</strong>: Open sourced July 2025, quickly gained <strong>15k+ stars</strong> (Studio ~7.3k, Loop ~2k initially). ByteDance-backed, frequent updates, active issues/discussions.</li><li><strong>n8n</strong>: Established 2019, <strong>130k+ stars</strong>, strong community, official forum/Discord, frequent updates, commercial sustainability.</li><li><strong>Dify</strong>: Launched March 2023, <strong>111k+ stars</strong>, LangGenius team, weekly releases, 290+ contributors, active Discord/WeChat.</li><li><strong>FastGPT</strong>: LabRing team, <strong>25k+ stars</strong>, active in China, frequent updates, commercial/community model.</li><li><strong>RAGFlow</strong>: consider future plans, technical evolution, and long-term maintenance guarantees. The following list summarizes the key roadmap highlights and sustainability prospects of the seven major platforms, helping readers quickly assess their long-term investment value and risks.</p><ul><li><strong>Coze Studio &amp; Loop</strong>: As part of ByteDance’s strategic initiative, Coze’s open sourcing is seen as the starting point for building an Agent ecosystem. According to press releases, ByteDance open sourced the Coze suite (Studio, Loop, Eino) to rally developers around Agent ecosystem development. The roadmap is expected to focus on <strong>lowering the barrier for Agent app creation, enriching plugins and templates, and integrating more large models</strong>. Coze Studio may integrate more algorithmic capabilities (supporting new tool protocols, more LLM interfaces), while Coze Loop may develop smarter Agent evaluation systems (such as automated prompt optimization and online learning). If ByteDance remains committed to the Agent ecosystem, it will likely continue investing resources to improve Coze and apply it in products like Feishu and Volcano Engine for validation. ByteDance’s abundant resources ensure strong sustainability for Coze—provided the Agent direction aligns with company strategy (currently, major tech firms are betting on <span class=\"glossary-term\">AGI<div class=\"glossary-tooltip-box\"><h4>AGI</h4><p>Artificial General Intelligence, a type of artificial intelligence (AI) that matches or exceeds</p></div></span>/Agent, so short-term abandonment is unlikely).</li><li><strong>n8n</strong>: n8n’s roadmap is gradually shifting from general automation to “AI-native automation.” Recent versions have introduced AI Nodes and LangChain integration, making AI-driven workflow construction easier. Future plans may include: <strong>AI assistants</strong> to help users build workflows (lowering usage barriers), more <strong>AI service integration nodes</strong>, and improved <strong>collaboration and version control</strong> features. As a commercial product, n8n’s roadmap also includes enterprise features (multi-environment deployment, audit logs, etc.) in new paid versions. With a clear revenue model (cloud subscriptions), n8n can continually improve its open source offering. Despite some concerns about its Fair-code license, n8n has found a practical balance between open source and commercial interests. With over four years of stable development, n8n is expected to grow steadily, especially in the AI automation space. For users, <strong>n8n’s sustainability is solid</strong>, making it a safe long-term choice—just keep up with new releases for the latest AI features.</li><li><strong>Dify</strong>: Dify’s website and documentation clearly outline its development direction, including expanding <strong>model support</strong> (adapting to the latest GPT-4.5 and domestic models), improving <strong>visual orchestration</strong>, and strengthening <strong>team collaboration and permissions</strong>. The roadmap includes plans like supporting file-type variables in Q3 2024 and continuously adding built-in tools and vector DB support. The LangGenius team is building Dify as both an open source and commercial product (cloud service and enterprise edition), so Dify will follow a <strong>community edition + paid enhanced edition</strong> dual path. As long as the LLM app market remains hot, Dify will keep evolving. Sustainability is ensured by key factors: a dedicated team, a large user base (180,000 developer community), and likely funding (not disclosed but probable). This shows Dify is a long-term project, not a fleeting trend. For long-term consideration, Dify is <strong>worth investing in</strong>—even if the community edition hits a bottleneck, commercial support is available to avoid isolation.</li><li><strong>FastGPT</strong>: Community discussions indicate FastGPT will continue to focus on <strong>enterprise knowledge base Q&amp;A</strong> scenarios. LabRing has launched a commercial edition, and the roadmap may place advanced features in the paid version to sustain open source iteration—similar to many ChatGPT open source alternatives. FastGPT may enhance <strong>large model adaptation</strong> (supporting more domestic models), <strong>knowledge base management</strong> (better data annotation and review), and <strong>workflow complexity</strong> (more node types). The community edition is already powerful; if the commercial edition is just plugin-based, it won’t affect the core. Sustainability depends on whether commercial revenue can support ongoing development. If LabRing’s cloud/private deployments sell well, the community edition will benefit; otherwise, open source maintenance may slow or seek integration with larger platforms. Given FastGPT’s popularity, community volunteers may also drive development. In summary, FastGPT is <strong>rapidly updated in the short term</strong>; medium- to long-term prospects depend on commercial success—cautiously optimistic.</li><li><strong>RAGFlow</strong>: InfiniFlow is likely to make RAGFlow a core part of its product suite, offering paid services (cloud hosting, enterprise support) around the open source engine. The roadmap already includes text, image, and audio parsing, and “Agentic RAG” support. Future directions may include evolving into a <strong>general <span class=\"glossary-term\">multimodal<div class=\"glossary-tooltip-box\"><h4>Multimodal</h4><p>Models or systems that process multiple data types (text, images, audio, etc.).</p></div></span> RAG platform</strong>, enabling unified Q&amp;A across various enterprise data formats, and improving <strong>explainability</strong> and <strong>accuracy</strong> (e.g., smarter scoring models). With its open source popularity, RAGFlow could become a de facto standard, attracting more community plugins and improvements. Sustainability depends on InfiniFlow’s ability to monetize enterprise editions (with GUI, visual management, etc.), which would fund ongoing engine development. Its 62k GitHub stars may also attract external partnerships or sponsorships. Notably, RAGFlow appeals to large enterprises and could be integrated or acquired by cloud vendors. Regardless, its Apache 2.0 license ensures that even if official support ends, the community can fork and continue development. Thus, RAGFlow has <strong>strong vitality</strong> and is suitable as part of a long-term solution.</li><li><strong>LangGraph</strong>: LangGraph’s future is closely tied to the LangChain ecosystem. Expect LangGraph to <strong>keep pace with LangChain updates</strong>, remain compatible with new interfaces, and add features based on user feedback (e.g., more workflow templates, simplified multi-Agent collaboration). LangChain has announced future support for a <strong>JS version</strong> (already has a JS repo) to broaden its application scope. As LangChain commercializes (LangSmith, private deployment), LangGraph will remain free as the open source component. Since LangChain aims to be the foundation for LLM app development, LangGraph is strategically important, and its roadmap will ensure <strong>stability and scalability</strong>, possibly including an official local visual tool (currently only online Studio). Sustainability is <strong>undeniable</strong>—LangChain is booming, well-funded, and has a large team. Choosing LangGraph means betting on the LangChain ecosystem, which is wise for long-term investment, though it requires keeping pace with LangChain’s evolution.</li><li><strong>Fabric</strong>: As a personal project, Fabric’s roadmap depends on the author’s vision and availability. Daniel aims to provide an “<strong>AI layer for daily life</strong>,” so future plans may include more <strong>skill modules</strong> (covering work and life), improved CLI experience, and possibly a simple GUI or web interface for easier onboarding. Without company backing, these are hobby-driven. Fabric will stay popular in the short term due to its novelty and utility, but long-term sustainability relies on <strong>community contributions</strong>. Other developers have already proposed improvements and forks (e.g., Home Assistant integration). If a collaborative community forms, Fabric could evolve and persist; if not, it may stagnate if the author loses interest. Thus, Fabric is best approached with caution and not relied on as a sole critical business dependency. It’s ideal for individuals and small teams to experiment with automation, but <strong>long-term reliance requires backup plans</strong> in case maintenance stops.</li></ul><p>In summary, most projects show positive roadmaps and growth potential. <strong>n8n, Dify, LangGraph</strong> have clear company-driven product evolution, ensuring ongoing improvements and long-term investment. <strong>Coze</strong> benefits from major corporate backing and will likely expand rapidly as long as the Agent direction remains relevant. <strong>RAGFlow</strong> is growing fast and may become a lasting open source pillar in the RAG field. <strong>FastGPT’s</strong> future depends on commercial viability—short-term usability is high, but long-term maintenance needs monitoring. <strong>Fabric</strong> is innovative but less certain due to lack of formal support. Teams looking to adopt these projects should choose based on their risk tolerance: try new projects for cutting-edge features, but select platforms with <strong>guaranteed ongoing support</strong> for mission-critical systems, and monitor community activity and version upgrades to adjust strategies promptly.</p><h2 id=\"summary\">Summary</h2><p>Open source AI Agent and workflow platforms each have unique strengths; selection should be based on actual needs. Enterprises and teams are advised to prioritize platforms like Coze Studio, Dify, and n8n, which have mature ecosystems, user-friendly visualization, and ongoing investment—ensuring long-term technical evolution and community support. Developers or those with special requirements can opt for flexible solutions like FastGPT, RAGFlow, or LangGraph, but should have sufficient technical expertise. Fabric is suitable for individuals to quickly experiment with automation. Always pay attention to license restrictions for SaaS/commercial use to ensure compliance. When choosing, focus on ecosystem activity, maintenance status, and future roadmap, and prioritize platforms with clear development plans and community support to reduce technical risk and benefit
